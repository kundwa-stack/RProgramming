Introduction

for this analysis, we will use the mtcars dataset, which contains information about 32 different car models

the objective is to investigate how different car characteristics influence fuel efficiency, measured in miles per gallon (MPG).

we will use: - dependent variable: mpg – fuel efficiency. - independent variable 1: wt – car weight. - independent variable 2: hp – horsepower. we want to understand whether car weight and horsepower can jointly explain differences in fuel efficiency

fitting the multiple linear regression model

now we fit a multiple linear regression model using car weight and horsepower to predict fuel efficiency

# Fitting the multiple linear regression model
model <- lm(mpg ~ wt + hp, data = data)

# Displaying the regression results
summary(model)

interpretation of the coefficients: 1. intercept (37.227): when weight and horsepower are both zero, the predicted MPG is approximately 37.227. This is mainly a mathematical starting point rather than a realistic car scenario. 2. weight (-3.878): holding horsepower constant, an increase of one unit in car weight (1,000 lbs) is associated with a decrease of approximately 3.878 MPG. 3. horsepower (-0.032): holding car weight constant, an increase of one horsepower is associated with a decrease of approximately 0.032 MPG. Both coefficients are negative, suggesting that heavier and more powerful cars tend to have lower fuel efficiency.

Evaluating the model

from the regression output, we examine the R-squared value and the p-values.

For this model, the approximate results are: - R-squared: 0.827 - Adjusted R-squared: 0.815

Interpretation:

The R-squared value of approximately 0.827 means that car weight and horsepower together explain about 82.7% of the variation in fuel efficiency among the cars in this dataset.

The remaining 17.3% is not explained by these two predictors and may be associated with other factors.

The overall regression model is statistically significant (p-value below 0.001).

Checking the regression diagnostics

we can use the standard R diagnostic plots to examine whether the regression assumptions are reasonably satisfied.

     ylab = "Cook's Distance")

Conclusion

the multiple linear regression analysis shows that car weight and horsepower are negatively associated with fuel efficiency. the results suggest that heavier cars and cars with higher horsepower generally consume more fuel per mile. the model explains approximately 82.7% of the variation in fuel efficiency, indicating that these two variables provide a good explanation of differences in MPG within this dataset
